### Organisation Membership & Profiles

- [ ] `MEMBER-1` **There is no way to join an organisation other than an admin adding you.** `org_memberships` rows are written by admin screens and by the claim flow; nothing models a person asking to join, and an organisation cannot say whether it accepts requests at all. **Decided 2026-09-03 — two modes, per organisation.** `invite` (the default): only an org admin creates a membership. `apply`: anybody may apply, and **applying does not make them a member** — the application sits pending until an org admin approves it, at which point the membership row is written. An admin can switch the org between the two. Needs: a mode on `organizations` (defaulting to `invite`, so existing orgs are unchanged), an application record with a pending / approved / declined state, the two actions to apply and to decide, notification to the org's admins, and a screen for the queue. Related: `PEOPLE-2` gates who may create a profile in the first place — the two are the same principle at two levels, since an approved application is what turns a person into a member rather than merely a profile. Logged rather than built because it does not touch the tournament work; raised 2026-09-03 during tournaments Phase 4.

- [ ] `MEMBER-2` **A person who belongs to no organisation has nowhere to be recorded.** `org_profiles` is per-org by design (`UNIQUE (org_id, identifier)`, the org's own name and number for that person), so somebody who is on the app but affiliated to nobody — an external official, a parent, a person who signed up through an invite and joined nothing — can only be recorded inside whichever org happened to touch them first. That org then owns their only person-record indefinitely, which is contrived in the general case. **Decided 2026-09-03 — a reserved organisation as a membership-free home for person records, under three constraints**, each of which is the difference between this being useful and being a privacy hole:
  - **Nothing ever writes an `org_memberships` row against it.** A membership row *is* a permission in this codebase: `getMembershipSnapshot` counts any current one, so "every user is a member of the global org" would hand everyone `org:{id}:members`, every team roster the org can see, and — since `isOrgMember` now selects the full search projection — everyone's `cellphone`, `birthdate` and `national_id`. That is `PEOPLE-1` reopened through the front door.
  - **It is excluded from the organisation directory and from member-scoped search**, for the same reason.
  - **It does not replace per-org profiles.** A person still needs a profile at their school to be a player there; the reserved-org row is the "not affiliated with anyone yet" record, not a canonical one. `AccessManager` already resolves a user into a *set* of profile ids, so several rows per human is the existing model rather than a new problem.

  Precedent for a reserved org exists: `org-system-admins` is special-cased in `AccessManager` and in the profile-collision check at [index.ts](file:///c:/Fred/Coding/SK/server/src/index.ts).

  **Also decided: "External" is derived, never stored.** A person with a profile in an org and no current membership is exactly what "external" means, and the people screen should label them so — but as a derived display value, not as a role. Storing it on `primary_role_id` would drift the moment they actually join, and creating a `role-org-external` *membership* would be the leak described above. `primary_role_id` is display-only today (no permission check reads it) and should stay that way.

  Related: `PEOPLE-2` (who may create a profile at all) and `MEMBER-1` (what turns a profile into a membership). Raised 2026-09-03 during tournaments Phase 4; logged rather than built because it is identity-model work, not tournament work.

- [ ] `MEMBER-3` **Nothing links a parent or guardian to a player, so there is no way to express who decides for a minor.** User research made this the clearest identity gap we have: in **both** interviews (Tableview FC 2026-09-09, Wynberg Boys' Primary 2026-09-11) every decision about a minor's availability, transport and attendance is made **with the adult, not the player** — while an adult player makes exactly the same decisions themselves. The schema has no way to say which of those two situations applies. `org_profiles` already anticipates parents (they are named in [identity_structure.md](file:///c:/Fred/Coding/SK/docs/identity_structure.md) §2 and §4 as ordinary members of a school directory) but no relationship connects one to a child.
  - **Designed 2026-09-12 in [identity_structure.md](file:///c:/Fred/Coding/SK/docs/identity_structure.md) §5.** A guardian is an ordinary `org_profile` with a `Guardian` role, and a new `profile_guardians` join table holds the link — many-to-many in both directions, because siblings share a guardian and a child can have two, with `start_date` / `end_date` mirroring `org_memberships` so a link lapses without losing history.
  - **Who answers for a player is derived, never stored** (§5.3): active guardian links mean the guardians answer, otherwise the player's own account does, so an adult player needs no special case. `birthdate` may decide whether registration *asks* for a guardian but must never decide the answer — the same principle as `MEMBER-2`'s "External is derived, never stored".
  - **Blocking risk — `member_count` would silently price parents.** [OrganizationManager.ts](file:///c:/Fred/Coding/SK/server/src/managers/OrganizationManager.ts) counts active `org_memberships` with a bare `COUNT(*)` (`member_count`, and `active_people`). Pricing counts **players and staff only** — parents and spectators are free — so the moment guardians hold a membership those counts include them. A school of ~800 learners has an estimated 1,200–1,600 parents. **Decided 2026-09-13: the priced count is an allow-list of counting roles** — `role-org-admin`, `role-org-staff` and `role-org-member`, which is the complete canonical list in `OrganizationManager.organizationRoles` today, so **the allow-list changes no number now**. Its value is that it inverts the default, so `role-org-guardian` and `role-trusted-contact` cannot silently become billable (§5.6).
  - **Latent bug to fix in the same change — a player could end up never counted.** [TeamManager.ts](file:///c:/Fred/Coding/SK/server/src/managers/TeamManager.ts) gives anyone added to a team a `role-org-member` membership, but **only when no active membership exists at all**. Once a non-counting role exists, a parent who is a `Guardian` and then joins a team as a social-side player or a coach already has a membership, so the check passes and they never receive `Member` — under-counted for pricing, and missing the baseline affiliation `role-org-member` grants. **Rule, agreed 2026-09-13: anyone involved with a team must hold at least one counting role**, so the check must test for a *counting* role rather than any role. Harmless today; only bites once `Guardian` exists.
  - **Related but separate — `role-trusted-contact` (§5.5).** A guardian, **or an adult player for themselves**, grants a grandparent, nanny, driver or partner access to one player's schedule. **Named 2026-09-13**: `Guest`, `Delegate`, `Nominee` and `Follower` were all rejected because each already means something else in this product (§5.5 records which). The principle is that a Trusted Contact **inherits the granter's view, never the granter's authority** — see by default, an optional off-by-default *respond* toggle for availability recorded as "on behalf of", and never another child, another person's contact details, consent flags, money or onward granting.
