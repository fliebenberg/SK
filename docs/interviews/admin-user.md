## How to use this guide

This is a **guide, not a script**. Ask what the conversation opens up; skip what has already been
answered. Sections are ordered so the participant talks about *their world* before they hear
anything about ours — do not describe ScoreKeeper until §14, or every answer after that point is
contaminated by it.

**Bias toward the concrete.** "Walk me through the last sports day you ran" beats "how do you
usually run a sports day", which beats "what would you want in a tool". Past behaviour is
evidence; predicted behaviour is not. If an answer comes back as a generalisation, ask for the
most recent actual instance.

**Follow the emotion.** Sighs, laughter and "oh, don't get me started" mark the expensive
problems. Stop and dig there, even if it costs you three later sections.

**Time budget** (~60 min): §1–3 warm-up, 10 min. §4–7 the core, 25 min. §8–13 as time allows,
20 min. §14–15 reactions and wrap-up, 5 min. **If you are running short, protect §4 and §6** —
they are the sections the product depends on.

**Referencing:** questions are cited as *section.question* — `4.3` is the third question in §4.
Every list item is written as `1.` in the source; the renderer numbers them, so numbering never
needs maintaining. Add new questions at the **end** of a section to keep existing references
stable. Indented bullets are probes — prompts for the interviewer, not questions to read aloud.

## 1. Background

1. What is your name?
1. What organisation do you work for or represent?
1. What is your role at the organisation?
   - Is sport your whole job, or part of a wider role?
1. How long have you been doing this, and how did you end up with it?
1. What sport(s) are you responsible for?
   - How many teams, roughly? How many participants?
   - Which age groups?
1. In a normal week during season, how much of your time goes to organising sport rather than
   coaching or teaching it?

## 2. How sport is organised here

1. How are the sports at your organisation managed?
   - Are there "leaders" for each sport?
   - Who do they report to? Who do *you* report to?
1. Who is responsible for organising sporting events?
1. Who else touches the admin side — fixtures, team lists, results?
   - Paid staff, volunteers, parents, coaches, senior participants?
1. Which parts can only you do, and which parts could someone else pick up tomorrow?
1. What happens when the person who normally handles this is away or leaves?
1. How does your organisation relate to any body above it — a league, union, federation or
   schools' association?
   - What do you have to report upward, and in what format?

## 3. The shape of your year

1. Walk me through a typical year of sport here — what happens when?
   - Which seasons run in parallel? When is the crunch?
1. What is the difference between the things that happen every week and the one-off events?
1. How does next season's calendar actually get made?
   - Do you start from last year's, or from scratch?
   - What gets copied, and what always has to be redone?
1. How far in advance is any of this settled, honestly?

## 4. The last multi-team event you ran — walk me through it *(core)*

> Anchor on one specific real event: a sports day, a festival, a tournament. Get the story in
> order, start to finish. Let them ramble; the detail is the point.

1. Which event should we talk about? Tell me what it was.
1. When did you first know it was happening, and what was the first thing you had to decide?
1. How did the other organisations get invited, and how did you know who was actually coming?
   - How long did confirmations take? Did anyone confirm and then not arrive?
1. How did you decide what would be played — which sports, which age groups, which teams?
1. What did you have to know before you could plan anything else?
1. What changed after you had planned it?
   - Withdrawals, extra teams, weather, a venue falling through?
   - How late was the latest change?
1. Which part of the whole thing took the longest?
1. What went wrong, and what did you do about it?
1. If you ran the same event again next year, what would you do differently?
1. Roughly how many hours went into it, over how many weeks?

## 5. Fixtures and scheduling

1. How do the actual fixtures get created — what does that look like physically?
   - Paper, whiteboard, spreadsheet, someone's head?
1. For that event, how many fixtures were there in the end?
1. What are the rules you are juggling when you build a draw?
   - Field and court availability, age groups, staggered starts, teams not playing back-to-back,
     travel, shared players, officials, lunch?
   - Which of those constraints is the one that actually breaks a schedule?
1. How long does building a draw take you, and how many versions do you go through?
1. Once it is built, how do people find out about it?
   - What does the published version look like? Who has to be sent it?
1. What happens when the schedule changes on the day?
   - How do people find out then?
1. Have you ever run something where teams progressed — pools into a knockout, quarters into
   semis? How did you handle who plays whom next?
   - How long between the last pool game and the first knockout match? Who worked the bracket
     out, and on what?
   - Who was standing around waiting for it, and how did they find out?
   - *If they have never run one — ask whether that is because nobody wants it, or because it
     is too much work to organise. Those are opposite answers for us.*
1. What do you do when you have to plan around a team whose identity you do not know yet
   ("winner of pool B", "whoever the third school sends")?

## 6. Running the day *(core)*

1. On the day itself, where are you and what are you holding?
1. How do scores get captured, fixture by fixture?
   - Who does it? A teacher, a scorer, a parent, the coach, a senior participant?
   - How reliable is that, really?
1. How do the scores get from wherever they are captured to wherever they need to be?
1. What is the phone signal and wifi like at your venues?
   - Has that ever caused a problem?
1. How quickly do results need to be visible, and to whom?
1. What happens when someone disagrees with a score, or a result is entered wrong?
   - Has that happened? Walk me through it.
1. Beyond the score, is anything else recorded during play?
   - Scorers, cards, times, individual performances, attendance?
   - Who ever looks at that afterwards?
1. What is the most stressful moment of a day like that?

## 7. Results, standings and winners

1. When the day is over, is there an overall winner, or just a set of results?
1. If the day is scored, how is it scored?
   - Who decided that, and when — beforehand, or argued about afterwards?
1. Do some results count for more than others?
   - Does a 1st team win outweigh a junior D win? By how much?
   - Does one sport count more than another?
1. Who works out the standings, and how long does it take?
1. What happens on a tie?
1. Where do the results end up afterwards?
   - Newsletter, noticeboard, social media, a report to the head, a spreadsheet nobody reopens?
1. Does anyone ever need last season's results back?
   - What for, and how easy is that to find?
1. For events that are *not* scored overall — does anyone still want a summary of how the day
   went?

## 8. Teams, participants and people

1. How are teams and squads recorded at the moment?
1. How often do those lists change during a season?
1. How do you handle someone who plays for more than one team, or plays up an age group?
1. Do coaches maintain their own team lists, or does it all come through you?
1. What about people who are not part of your organisation — officials, referees, opposition
   coaches? Do you need to record them anywhere?
1. What information about participants are you holding, and where does it live?
   - Contact details, medical, consent forms, photographs?
1. Are there rules you have to follow about participant data or photographs, particularly for
   minors?
   - Where do those rules come from — your own policy, the school, the association, law?
   - Has that ever stopped you using something?
1. How many participants are there across the organisation, and would you know that number without
   looking it up?
   - Does it hold steady through the year, or move with the season?
   - Where does the real number live — a list, a system, someone's head?
   - Is anyone counted twice, or missed, because of the way it is kept?
1. And the adults — how many people are involved in running the sport here?
   - Coaches, team managers, officials, admin staff, regular volunteers?
   - How many of those would need to be *in* a system like this, rather than just told things?
   - Does that number move much between seasons?

## 9. Communication

1. Who needs to be told what, and when?
   - Participants, parents, coaches, other organisations, spectators, your own management?
1. What do you use to tell them?
1. Which of those messages generates the most back-and-forth?
1. What question do you get asked over and over that you wish people could just look up?
1. Do parents or supporters ever ask for scores while a game is still going?
1. Do people outside your organisation ever need to see your fixtures or results?
1. How do supporters find out fixtures and results — parents who are not at the match, family,
   old boys and girls, the opposition's supporters?
   - What is public, and what only reaches people already on a list?
   - Who posts it, and how long after the event?
   - Do you hear from people who went looking and could not find it?
1. Is there anyone who wants your fixtures or results that you have no good way of reaching?
   - Do you have any idea how many people are actually looking?

## 10. What you use today

1. What tools or systems do you use for all of this now?
   - Walk me through where each thing actually lives.
1. Which of those works well? What would you not want to give up?
1. What have you tried and stopped using? Why did it not stick?
   - How long did it last, and was there a moment you knew it was over?
   - Did you drop it, or did everyone else quietly stop and you gave up chasing them?
   - Is it still technically in use by somebody, or still being paid for?
1. Is there anything you are required to use that you would not choose?
1. What lives only in one person's head, or one person's laptop?
1. If you had to hand the whole job over tomorrow, what would be hardest for the next person to
   pick up?
1. Where do you end up typing the same information more than once?

## 11. Delegation, access and trust

1. If someone else could do part of this, what would you hand over first?
1. What would you never hand over?
1. Would you be comfortable letting the person who runs one sport edit only that sport, without
   giving them the run of the whole event?
   - *Use a sport they actually run and, if they have named one, a real person. This lands very
     differently as "the netball convenor" than as "a convenor".*
   - Is that person usually staff, a volunteer, or from another organisation?
1. Has anyone ever changed something they should not have? What happened?
   - What did they change — and were they supposed to have access at all, or was it someone who
     legitimately could and should not have?
   - How did you find out? Did anything tell you, or did somebody just notice?
   - How long was it wrong for, and did anyone act on the wrong version before it was caught?
   - What changed afterwards — a rule, an access change, a conversation, or nothing?
1. How do people get access to your current systems, and what happens when they leave?
1. If a fixture or result changes, does anyone need to know *who* changed it?

## 12. Working with other organisations

1. Who is your counterpart at the other organisations you deal with — what is their role?
1. How do you coordinate with them today?
1. How much of the arranging happens between the two of you personally, rather than through any
   system?
1. If a tool required those other organisations to sign up before you could use it properly, how
   realistic is that?
   - What would happen if only half of them did?
1. Are there organisations you deal with that have almost no admin capacity at all?

## 13. Constraints and adoption

1. If you wanted a new tool for this, who decides — you, a head, a committee, a governing body?
1. Is there a budget for this kind of thing, and roughly what scale?
1. What devices would you and your people actually be using — phones, tablets, a shared laptop, a
   desktop in an office?
   - Are there IT restrictions on what can be installed or accessed?
1. How comfortable are the people around you with new software, honestly?
1. What would make you abandon a tool in the first month?
1. What would have to be true for something like this to be worth the effort of switching?

## 14. Reactions to the concept *(only after the above)*

> Now describe ScoreKeeper briefly and plainly. Then get reactions. Push back on enthusiasm —
> polite interest is not evidence. Ask "when did you last have that problem?" rather than "would
> you use this?"

1. Having heard that — what does it remind you of, and where does it not fit what you do?
1. What is missing that you would need on day one?
1. Which part of what I described would you actually not use?
1. *(Delegation)* An event has an organiser — a named person, not a job title — who can run that
   event and nothing else, and who can hand one sport to a convenor. Does that match how
   responsibility works here?
   - *Put the name of whoever they said they would hand something over to back to them. Would
     you give it to that person?*
1. *(Weighting)* If the app let you say a 1st team result counts for more than a junior result,
   would you use that, or is it more argument than it is worth?
   - *Anchor it to how they told you their last event was actually scored — or to the argument
     they described about how it should have been.*
1. *(Unscored days)* For a day with no agreed points system, would a plain summary — games played,
   won, drawn, lost per organisation — be useful, or is that noise?
   - *If they described a day that was never scored overall, ask about that day specifically.*
1. *(Provisional entrants)* How useful is being able to build a draw before you know who is
   coming, using placeholders you fill in later?
   - *Use their own story — the confirmations that came in late, or the team that said yes and
     then never arrived.*
1. *(Reuse)* How much of last year's event would you want to copy into this year's?
   - *They already told you what gets copied from last year's calendar and what always has to be
     redone. Read it back and ask whether that is the right split.*
1. What would you have to see before you would trust this with a real event rather than a practice
   run?
1. If this existed and worked exactly as described, what would you stop doing?

## 15. Wrap-up

1. What should I have asked you that I did not?
1. Is there anything you could show me — a spreadsheet, a draw, a group chat — that would explain
   this better than describing it?
1. Who else should I be talking to?
   - Someone in a very different kind of organisation? Someone who does the job on the day rather
     than plans it?
1. Would you be willing to look at something early and tell us where it is wrong?
1. Can I come back to you with follow-up questions?
