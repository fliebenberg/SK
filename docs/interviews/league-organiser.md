## How to use this guide

This is a **guide, not a script**. Ask what the conversation opens up; skip what has already been
answered. Sections are ordered so the participant talks about *their world* before they hear
anything about ours — do not describe ScoreKeeper until §14, or every answer after that point is
contaminated by it.

**Who this guide is for.** Someone who runs a competition **across organisations they do not own** —
a local football association, a schools' union, a district or provincial body, a league committee.
This is the layer *above* the club admin, and it is a different job: they cannot instruct a club's
volunteers, they depend on clubs to submit things on time, and their authority is rules and
sanctions rather than management. The [admin guide](admin-user.md) assumes one organisation running
its own events — do not reuse it here.

**Bias toward the concrete.** "Walk me through how last season's fixtures got built" beats "how do
you usually build fixtures", which beats "what would you want in a tool". Past behaviour is
evidence; predicted behaviour is not.

**Follow the emotion.** With a league it usually sits in one of three places: clubs who do not
submit results, discipline, and the week the season's draw is built. Dig where the sighs are.

**The size question here is different.** A league's "how big are you" is clubs, teams, registered
participants *and* the club officials it deals with — four numbers, not two. §2.6–2.8 asks for
them. Do not raise pricing while asking.

**Time budget** (~60 min): §1–3 warm-up, 10 min. §4–5 and §8 the core, 25 min. §6–7, §9–13 as time
allows, 20 min. §14–15 reactions and wrap-up, 5 min. **If you are running short, protect §4, §5
and §8** — building the draw, changing it in-season, and getting results back are the three things
this product would live or die on.

**Referencing:** questions are cited as *section.question* — `4.3` is the third question in §4.
Every list item is written as `1.` in the source; the renderer numbers them, so numbering never
needs maintaining. Add new questions at the **end** of a section to keep existing references
stable. Indented bullets are probes — prompts for the interviewer, not questions to read aloud.

## 1. Background

1. What is your name?
1. What is the body called, and what does it cover — geographically and by sport?
1. What is your role in it?
   - Is it paid or voluntary? Full-time, part-time, or evenings and weekends?
1. How long have you been doing this, and how did you end up with it?
1. How many other people work on running the competition, and what do they each do?
1. In a normal week during season, how many hours does this actually take?
   - Which week of the year is the worst?

## 2. What the league is, and who is in it

1. How is the body structured — committee, board, elected officers, paid staff?
1. How does a club become part of your league, and can you refuse one?
1. What do clubs get from being affiliated, and what do they owe you in return?
1. How many divisions or sections do you run, and how do they relate to each other?
   - Age groups, tiers, men's and women's, development vs competitive?
1. Where do you sit in the pyramid — who is above you, and who is below?
   - What do you have to report upward, in what format, and to what deadline?
1. How many **clubs** are affiliated?
1. How many **teams** does that come to across all divisions?
1. And **people** — roughly how many registered participants, and how many club officials do you
   deal with directly?
   - Would you know those numbers without looking them up? Where does the real number live?
   - Is anyone counted twice — someone registered at two clubs, or a coach who is also a player?
1. How much do the clubs differ from each other in how well organised they are?
   - Who is the best-run club you deal with, and who is the one that worries you?

## 3. The shape of the season

1. Walk me through a season from your side — what happens when?
   - When does the planning start relative to the first fixture?
1. What has to be finished before you can start building fixtures at all?
1. When is registration open, and what is the deadline?
1. What are the fixed points in the year you cannot move — school terms, holidays, cup weekends,
   representative fixtures, the AGM?
1. How much of next season is a copy of last season?
   - What gets carried over, and what has to be rebuilt from scratch every time?
1. When does the season *actually* end, as opposed to when it was supposed to?

## 4. Building the season's fixtures *(core)*

> Anchor on the most recent season. Get the story in order, start to finish, and find out how long
> each step really took.

1. Who physically builds the fixture list, and on what?
1. Walk me through how last season's fixtures got built — start at the first thing you did.
1. What do you have to know before you can start?
   - Final list of entrants, grounds available, teams sharing a ground, kit clashes?
1. How many fixtures is it, across everything, in a season?
1. What are the constraints you are juggling?
   - Home and away balance, clubs sharing a ground, teams that cannot play Sunday mornings,
     travel distance, pitch availability, floodlights, teams with two sides in the same division?
   - Which one of those actually breaks a draw?
1. How long does it take, honestly, from start to a published fixture list?
   - How many versions do you go through before it is right?
1. What do you do about a club that enters late, or withdraws after the draw is built?
   - How late is too late? Has a club ever pulled out mid-season?
   - What happens to the fixtures they had already played?
1. How do the fixtures get published, and who has to be told directly?
1. Once published, how often does the list change over the season, roughly?
1. Do you also allocate grounds and kick-off times, or do clubs do that themselves?
1. Do you run any competition that is *not* a straight league — a cup, a knockout, a festival?
   - Who works out the bracket, and when? How quickly after the previous round?
1. Are there fixtures where you do not know who is playing yet — cup rounds, promotion play-offs?
   - How do you plan around that?

## 5. When the season changes underneath you *(core)*

> This is where a league's work actually lives. Slow down here.

1. What are the reasons a fixture gets moved or called off?
   - Weather, ground unavailable, a club cannot raise a side, representative call-ups, COVID-era
     habits that stuck?
1. Walk me through the last postponement. Who told whom, and in what order?
1. Who has the authority to postpone — the club, the referee, you?
1. How does a postponed fixture get rearranged, and who chases it?
   - How long does it typically sit unplayed?
   - What is your leverage if a club will not agree a date?
1. What happens to the table while a fixture is outstanding?
   - Do you show games in hand? Does anyone get confused by that?
1. What happens when a club simply does not turn up?
   - Walkover, forfeit, points awarded, a fine? Who decides?
1. Has a club ever folded mid-season? What did you do with their results?
1. How much of your week is spent on this compared with everything else?

## 6. Registration and eligibility

1. How does a participant get registered to play in your competition?
1. What do you need from them, and who checks it?
   - Age verification, photographs, identity documents, consent, medical?
1. Where does that information live, and who can see it?
1. What are the rules you have to follow about participant data — particularly for minors?
   - Where do those rules come from: your own policy, the national body, the law?
   - Has that ever stopped you doing something you wanted to do?
1. How do transfers between clubs work?
1. How do you catch an ineligible participant — someone unregistered, suspended, or playing up or
   down an age group?
   - Has that happened? What was the consequence for the result?
1. Who actually checks the team sheet against the registration list, and when?
1. How much of the season is spent chasing clubs for registration paperwork?

## 7. Officials

1. How are referees and other officials appointed to fixtures?
   - Who does the appointing, and on what?
1. Which fixtures do you supply officials for, and which do the clubs cover themselves?
   - What decides that split?
1. How do you know how many officials you have available on a given weekend?
1. Are officials graded or ranked, and does that affect what they get appointed to?
1. How does an official find out about an appointment, and how do they confirm it?
1. What happens when an official does not arrive?
1. How are officials paid, and by whom?
1. Where do new officials come from, and is that number going up or down?
1. What do you need back from an official after a fixture, and how does it reach you?

## 8. Results, verification and the table *(core)*

1. After a fixture is played, what is supposed to happen?
   - Walk me through it from the final whistle to the table being right.
1. Who submits the result, and by when?
1. What exactly has to be submitted besides the score?
   - Team sheet, cards, scorers, times, the official's report?
1. What form does that take — paper, photograph, a form, a portal?
1. How many clubs miss the deadline in a normal week, and what do you do about it?
1. Does anyone check the result is *right*, or is it taken on trust?
1. What happens when the two clubs disagree about the score?
   - Has that happened? Walk me through it.
1. Who updates the table, and how long after the last fixture is it correct?
1. What are your tie-break rules, and how often do they actually get used?
1. Do any results count for more than others — bonus points, penalties, deductions?
1. How is promotion and relegation decided, and when is it final?
1. Has a table ever been wrong in public? What happened?
1. How far back do you keep results, and does anyone ever ask for old ones?

## 9. Discipline and disputes

1. What happens to a card after a fixture — who records it, and who acts on it?
1. How are suspensions tracked, and how does a club find out one of theirs is suspended?
   - What stops a suspended player being picked next week?
1. How often does a club dispute something, and what form does that take?
1. Who hears a protest, and how long does it take to resolve?
1. What sanctions do you have available, and which one do you actually use?
1. Where is all of this recorded?
1. How much of your time does this take relative to running the competition itself?

## 10. Communication with clubs

1. Who at each club is your point of contact, and is that the right person?
1. How do you communicate with clubs — and how do they communicate with you?
1. When something changes, how do you know the message actually landed?
1. Which message causes the most back-and-forth?
1. What question do clubs ask you over and over that they could look up?
1. Do you ever need to reach past the club to participants, parents or spectators directly?
   - Can you? Should you?
1. How do supporters and the public find out fixtures and results?
   - What is public, and what only reaches affiliated clubs?
1. Does anyone outside the league use your results — press, schools, a national body, betting?

## 11. What you use today

1. What systems do you use to run the competition?
   - Walk me through where each thing actually lives.
1. Who chose them, and how long have you had them?
1. Which parts work well? What would you not want to give up?
1. Which parts do you work around, and how?
1. What have you tried and stopped using? Why did it not stick?
1. Is anything you use imposed on you from above, rather than chosen?
1. What lives only in one person's head or one person's laptop?
1. If you were hit by a bus tomorrow, what would the next person struggle most to pick up?
1. Where do you end up entering the same information more than once?
1. Do the clubs use the same system you do, or something of their own?

## 12. Money and obligations

1. How is the league funded — affiliation fees, match fees, fines, sponsorship, a grant?
1. Who pays for the systems you use now, and roughly what scale of money is that?
1. Who would decide to adopt something new — you, a committee, a vote of the clubs, the body above
   you?
   - How long would that take, and when in the year could it even be discussed?
1. Are you required by anyone above you to use a particular system or report in a particular
   format?
1. What are you obliged to keep records of, and for how long?

## 13. The clubs beneath you

> The league is one user; its clubs are many. This section is about whether the league can bring
> them, and what happens to the ones that cannot follow.

1. If you adopted a new system, could you require the clubs to use it?
   - Has the league ever mandated something like that before? How did it go?
1. Which clubs would take it up immediately, and which would still be on paper a year later?
1. What proportion of your clubs have anyone you would call an administrator?
1. What do clubs do today that duplicates what you do — keeping their own fixtures, their own
   results, their own registers?
1. Do clubs ever complain about having to enter the same thing into two places?
1. If half your clubs adopted something and half did not, would that be workable or worse than
   nothing?
1. Who among your clubs would tell you honestly whether something was working?

## 14. Reactions to the concept *(only after the above)*

> Now describe ScoreKeeper briefly and plainly. Then get reactions. Push back on enthusiasm —
> polite interest is not evidence. Ask "when did you last have that problem?" rather than "would
> you use this?"

1. Having heard that — what does it remind you of, and where does it not fit what you do?
1. What is missing that you would need on day one?
1. Which part of what I described would you actually not use?
1. *(Fixture generation)* If the draw for a whole season could be generated from the entrants and
   the constraints you described, and then edited by hand, how much of your week does that take
   back?
   - *Read their own §4.6 answer back to them before asking.*
   - Which of your constraints would it have to understand before you would trust it?
1. *(Results in, not typed up)* If results arrived from the clubs already structured — score, team
   sheet, cards — instead of as a photograph or a form, what changes for you?
   - *Anchor to what they described in §8.3–8.4.*
1. *(The league / club split)* If the league held the competition and the clubs held their own
   teams and participants, with the same data serving both — does that match how responsibility
   actually divides here?
   - Who should be able to change a fixture: you, the club, or both?
   - Who should be able to change a result after it is submitted?
1. *(Mandate)* Would you be willing to require your clubs to use it, or would it have to earn its
   way in club by club?
   - *Use their §13.1 answer. If they said they could mandate it, ask what would make them
     actually do it.*
1. *(Public results)* If every fixture and table were public and shareable without a login, is that
   a benefit or a problem for you?
1. What would you have to see before you would trust this with a live season rather than a trial?
1. If this existed and worked exactly as described, what would you stop doing?
1. What would make you abandon it in the first month?

## 15. Wrap-up

1. What should I have asked you that I did not?
1. Is there anything you could show me — a fixture spreadsheet, the portal, a results email, the
   discipline register — that would explain this better than describing it?
1. Who else should I be talking to?
   - Someone at the body above you? A club secretary who deals with you every week? One of your
     best-run clubs and one of your worst?
1. Would you be willing to look at something early and tell us where it is wrong?
1. Can I come back to you with follow-up questions?
