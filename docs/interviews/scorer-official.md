## How to use this guide

Same rules as the other guides: concrete over general, follow the emotion, nothing about
ScoreKeeper until §11.

**Establish which kind of scorer they are (§1.3) and adapt.** An appointed club scorer, a referee
who also records, a coach scoring their own side, and a parent handed a clipboard five minutes
before kick-off are four different jobs with four different failure modes. If they are the coach,
run [coach-team-manager.md](coach-team-manager.md) instead and use §5–7 here as the deep dive.

**This is the one guide where you should ask to see the artefact.** A scoresheet, a scorebook, a
photo of a completed card — ask early (§1.7) and refer to it throughout. What is printed on the
sheet tells you what the sport considers worth capturing far more reliably than what anyone
remembers to say.

**Watch for the gap between what is supposed to happen and what happens.** Scorers are usually
working under time pressure, in weather, with one hand. Ask about the last match, not the
procedure, and ask what they do when they fall behind.

**Time budget** (~45 min): §1–3, 8 min. §4–7 the core, 25 min. §8–10, 10 min. §11–12, 5 min.
**If you are running short, protect §5 and §6.**

**Referencing:** questions are cited as *section.question* — `4.3` is the third question in §4.
Every list item is written as `1.` in the source; the renderer numbers them. Add new questions at
the **end** of a section to keep existing references stable. Indented bullets are probes, not
questions to read aloud.

## 1. Background

1. What is your name?
1. What organisation are you connected to, and how?
1. How would you describe your role when a match is on?
   - Appointed scorer, referee, coach, parent, volunteer?
1. How did you end up doing this?
   - Volunteered, asked, part of a job, nobody else would?
1. How many matches do you do in a season, roughly?
1. Were you trained or shown how, or did you work it out?
   - Is there a qualification or accreditation for this kind of scoring?
1. Do you have a scoresheet or scorebook from a recent match you could show me?
1. How big is the organisation you score for?
   - Roughly how many teams and players, and how many adults involved?
   - A rough number is fine. If you do not know, who would?

## 2. The sport and what counts

1. Which sport or sports do you score?
1. In simple terms, how does scoring work in that sport?
1. What has to be recorded for a match to count as properly recorded?
1. Is there anything you record that nobody ever seems to look at?
1. Is there anything people ask for afterwards that you do not record?
1. Do different competitions or age groups want different things recorded?

## 3. Before the match

1. When do you find out you are scoring a particular match?
   - How much notice do you get?
1. What do you need to have before you can start?
   - Team sheets, player names and numbers, the right form, a pen?
1. Where do the team lists come from, and are they ever wrong or late?
1. What happens if you do not have the names — do you score by number, or by shirt colour?
1. How long before the start do you need to be there?

## 4. Your situation while scoring

1. Where are you physically during a match?
   - Sideline, in a box, at a table, standing in the rain?
1. What are you using — paper, a scorebook, a phone, a tablet, a laptop?
   - Whose device is it?
1. Are you sitting or standing? Do you have a surface?
1. Are you doing anything else at the same time?
   - Running the clock, watching your own child, refereeing, coaching, managing subs?
1. What is the phone signal and wifi like where you do this?
   - What do you do when there is none?
1. What happens to your set-up in bad weather, or when it gets dark?
1. How long is a match, and are you doing more than one in a day?

## 5. Scoring the match *(core)*

1. Walk me through the last match you scored, from the first whistle.
1. What happens in the moment a point is scored — what do you actually do, in order?
1. How much time do you have before the next thing happens?
1. When does the game get ahead of you?
   - What happens in your sport in a fast passage of play?
   - What do you do to catch up?
1. What do you do if you miss something entirely?
1. Are you ever unsure what just happened?
   - Who do you ask? Do you stop the game, or guess and fix it later?
1. How do you correct a mistake?
   - Crossed out, tippex, a new line, erased? What does the sheet look like afterwards?
1. Has anything you scored ever turned out to be wrong after the fact?
   - How was it found, and what happened?
1. Are there parts of the game where you deliberately stop recording detail?
1. Which sport or which level is hardest to score, and why?

## 6. The clock, and who has authority *(core)*

1. Who runs the clock — you, the referee, someone else?
1. If more than one person is timing, do the times ever disagree?
   - What is the official one?
1. How are stoppages handled — injuries, half time, delays?
1. If your sheet and the referee disagrees with it, who wins?
1. If your sheet and the opposition's scorer disagree, what happens?
   - Do you compare at any point during the match, or only at the end?
   - Has that gone wrong? Tell me about it.
1. Does anyone sign off the result at the end?
1. Who is the official record — your sheet, or something else?
1. Have you ever been put under pressure to record something a particular way?
   - Who from — a coach, a parent, an official, someone from your own club?
   - What did they actually want, and what did you do?
   - Would anyone have been able to tell afterwards that it had been changed?
   - *If they say no, try it in the third person — do you know it happening to anyone else?
     People will tell you about a colleague long before they tell you about themselves.*

## 7. More than one person scoring

1. Is anyone else scoring the same match?
   - Both sides, an official scorer plus a coach, a parent keeping their own?
1. Do you compare with them, and when?
1. Have you ever had two people entering into the same system at once?
   - What happened?
1. If someone else could undo or change something you had entered, how would you feel about that?
   - Should you be asked first? Should you be able to see who changed it?
1. In your sport, is there ever more than one independent judge scoring the same performance?
   - How are their scores combined?

## 8. After the match

1. What happens to the scoresheet when the match ends?
1. Who do you have to give the result to, and how fast?
1. Does anyone check it?
1. Do you enter it into anything afterwards?
   - How long after? Where? Do you have to re-type what you already wrote?
1. What happens to the paper?
1. Have you ever been asked about a match weeks or months later?
   - Could you find it?
1. Does anybody ever thank you or tell you the result was used for anything?

## 9. What goes wrong

1. What is the most stressful part of scoring a match?
1. What is the mistake that is easiest to make in your sport?
1. Tell me about the worst scoring experience you have had.
1. What would make you say no next time you were asked?
1. What would make the job noticeably easier?
   - Ask this open, before offering anything.

## 10. Tools and technology

1. Has anyone ever asked you to score on a phone, tablet or computer?
   - How did that go?
1. What was good about it, and what made you want the paper back?
1. Do you have a phone with you while scoring? Would you be willing to use it for this?
   - Battery, data, screen in sunlight, gloves, rain?
1. Who would provide the device — you, the club, the school?
1. How comfortable are you with new apps, honestly?
1. Are there other scorers you know who would refuse outright?

## 11. Reactions to the concept *(only after the above)*

> Describe ScoreKeeper's scoring side briefly and plainly. Then get reactions. Be especially
> sceptical of enthusiasm here — agreeing that an app sounds good is very different from choosing
> it over a scorebook with cold hands.

1. Having heard that — where would that fit, and where would it fall apart for you?
1. What would have to be true for you to leave the paper at home?
1. *(Speed)* How many taps is too many for one score?
   - *They described the passage of play where the game gets ahead of them. Ask about that one,
     not about a typical score.*
1. *(Detail)* If the app asked you for more than the score — who scored it, why a penalty was
   given, which player was carded — would you have time for that? Would you want to?
   - Is there a level of detail where you would just stop?
1. *(Undo)* If you made a mistake and had to ask the other scorer to approve undoing it, with a
   short time limit — sensible, or infuriating?
   - *Compare it directly with how they told you they fix a mistake on paper today, and how long
     that takes them.*
1. *(Offline)* If you had no signal for a whole match and the app saved everything until you did,
   would you trust that?
   - *Use their own answer about signal where they score. If it is fine there, ask about the
     worst venue they go to.*
1. *(Live)* If everything you entered appeared instantly to parents and spectators watching from
   elsewhere — how does that change how you would work?
   - Would you feel watched? Would you slow down to be sure?
   - *If they described being leaned on to record something a particular way, this is where to
     bring it back — an audience makes that pressure worse, not better.*
1. What would you still want on paper regardless?
1. Would you rather do this alongside a paper sheet for a season, or switch outright?
   - If alongside — what would you be checking the app against, and would you really compare the
     two after every match?
   - What would have to happen before you stopped double-writing?

## 12. Wrap-up

1. What should I have asked you that I did not?
1. Can I take a photo of that scoresheet?
1. Who else should I talk to?
   - Someone who scores a different sport? Someone who has been doing it for twenty years?
     Someone who gave up?
1. Would you be willing to try something early and tell us where it is wrong?
1. Can I come back to you with follow-up questions?
