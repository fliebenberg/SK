## How to use this guide

Shorter than the others — aim for **30–40 minutes**. Spectators have less to tell you about
process and more to tell you about attention: what they check, when, and what they do with it.

**This persona lies to you more than the others**, not deliberately. Everyone says they would love
live scores and a place to chat about the team. Almost nobody opens a fifth app. So the whole
guide is built to get at *current behaviour* before appetite: §7 asks where the conversation
already happens, and it is the single most useful section in here. Someone who is already in three
WhatsApp groups about their child's rugby is telling you something real; someone who says a fan
chat "sounds nice" is telling you nothing.

**Ask "when did you last…" relentlessly.** Every claimed habit should be pinned to a specific
occasion. "Do you check scores?" gets a yes from everyone. "When did you last check a score you
weren't at?" gets you the truth.

**§1 opens wide, then narrows.** It asks about sport in their life generally — including the
professional team they follow — before it gets to the child or club side this interview is
actually about. That is deliberate. How somebody follows a pro team is *observed behaviour*, and
it is the standard they will unconsciously measure school and club sport against. But it is
context, not a requirement — nobody is building SuperSport for an under-14 netball fixture. Move
through it briskly and do not let the interview settle there.

**Establish the relationship before §2 (§1.6).** A parent of an under-12, an old boy who watches
the first team, and a partner dragged along on Saturdays want almost nothing in common. §1.6 is
the pivot: everything from §2 onwards is about whoever they name there, so make sure both of you
know who that is.

**Time budget:** §1–2, 7 min. §3–5, 10 min. §6–7 the core, 12 min. §8–9, 6 min. §10–11, 5 min.

**Referencing:** questions are cited as *section.question* — `4.3` is the third question in §4.
Every list item is written as `1.` in the source; the renderer numbers them. Add new questions at
the **end** of a section to keep existing references stable. Indented bullets are probes, not
questions to read aloud.

## 1. Background

1. What is your name?
1. Where does sport sit in your life — do you play it, watch it, follow it, or is it mostly
   through other people?
1. Which sports do you follow, and is there one you would call yours?
   - Professional, national, local, school or club?
   - Is the one you most enjoy watching the same one you follow most closely?
1. Which team is *your* team?
   - How do you keep up with them — where did their last result actually reach you?
   - An app, a broadcaster, social media, a group chat, someone telling you?
1. Were you ever a player, coach, official or organiser yourself?
   - Which sport, how far did you go, and how long ago?
1. And closer to home — whose matches do you actually turn up to?
   - A child, a partner, a club side, a school, an old team of your own?
   - *Everything from here on is about whoever they name. Say so, and write it at the top.*
1. How did that start, and how long has it been going on?
1. Are you following more than one team or child at once?
   - Do they ever clash?
1. How big is the club or school you follow?
   - Roughly how many players or pupils? Does it feel like a big place or a small one?
   - An estimate is fine — I am after the size of the world you are following, not the exact
     figure.

## 2. How much of it you actually see

1. In a normal season, how many matches do you get to?
1. How many do you miss that you would have liked to see?
   - What stops you — work, distance, clashes, cost?
1. Is there a match you would never miss?
1. Does anyone else in your family follow it too?
   - Do you end up relaying it to them?

## 3. When you are there

1. Walk me through the last match you attended, from setting off.
1. How did you know when and where it was?
1. Did you know who was playing before you got there?
1. During the game, what do you have in your hand?
1. Do you ever not know what the score is while you are standing there?
   - How do you find out? Is there a scoreboard?
1. Do you ever check your phone during a match? What for?
1. Do you take photos or video?
   - What do you do with them?
1. What do you talk about with the other people on the sideline?

## 4. When you are not there

1. Think of the last match you could not get to. How did you find out what happened?
   - Who told you, and how long after?
1. Did you try to find out while it was still going on?
   - What did you actually do?
1. How much do you want to know while it is happening?
   - Just the score, or more?
1. Is waiting until afterwards fine, or does it bother you?
1. Has anyone ever sent you updates from the sideline?
   - Was that useful or annoying?
1. If your child is playing and you cannot be there, is that different from a team you support?

## 5. Finding out what is on

1. How do you find out about upcoming fixtures?
   - What arrives, from whom?
1. Do you always know the time and place, or do you have to ask?
1. Have you ever gone to the wrong place, or the wrong time? What happened?
   - Where did it break down — nobody told you, you were told too late, you were reading an old
     message, or you assumed it was the same as last time?
   - Who did you try to ask once you realised? Did you get hold of them?
   - What do you check now that you did not check before?
1. What happens when something is moved or cancelled?
   - How do you find out? Tell me about the last time.
1. Is there anything you would like to know in advance that you never get told?
   - Whether your child is playing, who the opposition is, directions, parking, what to bring?

## 6. Afterwards *(core)*

1. After a match, what do you want to know?
1. Where do you get it?
1. Do you look up results of matches you had nothing to do with?
   - Other teams at the same school, the age group above, the opposition's other games?
1. Does a league table or standings matter to you?
   - Do you know where your team sits right now?
1. Do you ever go back and look at older results?
   - Last season, a whole season's record, how many your child played?
1. Is there anything about the season you would like a record of and do not have?
1. What do you share with other people, and where?

## 7. Where the conversation already happens *(core)*

> The most predictive section in this guide. Do not mention any product idea here — you are mapping
> what already exists. Ask them to open their phone and count if they are willing.

1. Where do people talk about this team at the moment?
   - Group chats, social media, in person, a club bar?
1. How many groups or chats are you in relating to this?
   - Who set them up? Who is in them?
1. What gets posted in them?
1. Do you post, or mostly read?
1. What is good about them, and what is annoying?
   - Volume, off-topic, arguments, people you cannot mute?
1. Has one ever gone wrong — an argument, someone said something about a child, someone had to
   step in?
   - What happened? Did anyone moderate it?
1. Do you follow the school or club on social media?
   - Do they post results? Is it any good?
1. Is there a conversation you would like to be part of that does not exist?
1. Are there people you would *not* want in a supporters' group?

## 8. Names, photos and children

> Keep this short and matter-of-fact. This is the guide where it matters most — the answers are
> about their own child, not about policy.

1. How do you feel about your child's name appearing alongside results publicly?
   - Does that change with age?
1. What about photographs?
1. Is there anything you would not want visible to people outside the school or club?
1. Have you ever been asked to give or withhold permission for any of this?
1. Would you want to control who can see what, or would you rather the school just decided?

## 9. Apps and attention

1. What apps do you check most days?
1. Do you use anything for sport already — a league app, a scores app, a club app?
   - Which do you keep, and which did you delete?
1. What made you delete one?
1. How do you feel about notifications?
   - What would you want to be told about immediately, and what would make you turn them off?
1. Would you make an account for something like this, or would you want it to just work?
1. Would you pay for anything here? Would you expect the school or club to?

## 10. Reactions to the concept *(only after the above)*

> Describe ScoreKeeper plainly, from a spectator's point of view. Then get reactions. Follow every
> yes with "when did you last want that?"

1. Having heard that — what would you use it for?
1. What would make you open it on a Saturday morning?
1. *(Live)* If you could watch the score update live from a match you were not at, when would you
   actually have used that this season?
   - *They described a match they could not get to and how they eventually found out. Use that
     one rather than letting them answer in general.*
1. *(Following)* Would you want to follow a team and be told when they play and how they did?
   - How often is too often to hear from it?
   - *They already told you what makes them turn notifications off. Hold them to it.*
1. *(Detail)* Beyond the score, would you want the detail — who scored, when, how the game went?
   - Or is that more than you need?
1. *(Community)* If there were a place in the app to talk with other supporters of the team — what
   is your honest reaction?
   - Would it be better or worse than the group chat you are already in?
   - Would you actually move, or would you end up in both?
   - Who should be allowed in — anyone, or only people connected to the club?
   - What would need to happen when someone says something out of line?
1. *(History)* Would a record of your child's season — matches played, results — be something you
   would want at the end of the year?
   - *If they named something about the season they wished they had a record of, offer that back
     specifically instead of asking in general.*
1. Which part of all that would you never touch?
1. What would make you stop using it after a week?

## 11. Wrap-up

1. What should I have asked you that I did not?
1. Is there anything on your phone you could show me — how you found the last result, a group
   chat — that would explain this better than describing it?
1. Who else should I talk to?
   - Someone who follows a different sport or age group? A grandparent? Someone who never goes?
1. Would you be willing to try something early and tell us what is wrong with it?
1. Can I come back to you with follow-up questions?
